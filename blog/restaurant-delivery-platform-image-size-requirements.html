U.S. Restaurant Image Guide

Restaurant Image Size Requirements for DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, Toast, Square, Clover & ezCater

A one-stop reference for image dimensions, aspect ratios, file limits, cropping rules, and the POS-sync issues that can unexpectedly change photos across your restaurant’s ordering channels.

DoorDashUber EatsGrubhubToastSquareCloverezCater

Restaurant image requirements are not standardized. A photo that looks perfect on one platform may be cropped, rejected, compressed, or reused differently on another.

This guide separates publicly verified platform requirements from practical recommendations. Platform interfaces and partner integrations change, so always treat the upload screen inside your own merchant account as the final authority.

Do not rely on an AI summary alone

Search summaries often combine old help-center pages, third-party recommendations, and placement-specific rules. For example, a Grubhub 2400 × 600 header recommendation is commonly repeated, but Grubhub’s public merchant page currently verifies its square menu and logo rules—not that header dimension. Use unverified dimensions only after checking your portal or confirming with support.

DoorDash Image Requirements

Menu item photos

  • Minimum: 1400 × 800 px
  • Aspect ratio: 16:9 landscape
  • Format: JPG, JPEG, or PNG
  • File size: under 16 MB
  • Creative: one clearly identifiable item, centered, no text overlays, borders, watermarks, or collages

Logo

  • 230 × 230 px
  • Square 1:1 works best
  • Under 2 MB

Header / carousel / hero

  • 1400 × 800 px minimum
  • Under 2 MB
  • DoorDash may render the asset at different crops on web and in the app, so keep important food and branding near the center
  • Use a scene or several dishes, but avoid a tiled collage

Source: DoorDash photo guidance.

2

Uber Eats Image Requirements

Cover photo

  • 2880 × 2304 px
  • 5:4 aspect ratio
  • JPEG
  • Centered, level, properly cropped, and representative of products customers can order
  • Avoid people unless you have appropriate image rights; do not let branding overpower the food

Menu item photos

  • JPG, PNG, or GIF
  • Maximum 10 MB
  • Width: 550–10,000 px
  • Height: 440–10,000 px
  • Show one item, in focus, well lit, and centered with enough margin for automatic cropping

Uber Eats crop warning

Item images may be displayed as square thumbnails even when the uploaded file is rectangular. Keep the dish inside a centered “safe zone” and do not place garnishes, packaging, or important details against the edges.

Sources: Uber merchant photo guidelines.

3

Grubhub Image Requirements

Publicly verified menu and logo rules

  • 1:1 square
  • At least 200 × 200 px
  • PNG
  • Logos require a background color; white is acceptable
  • Non-logo images must be food—coupons, specials graphics, and restaurant interiors/exteriors may be rejected
  • Images are screened for unsafe content and copyright matches

Grubhub header guidance varies

Some partner guides recommend a 4:1, 2400 × 600 JPEG header. Because that number is not currently stated on Grubhub’s public merchant criteria page, confirm the required dimensions in your Grubhub portal before creating a final header export.

Source: Grubhub menu and photo criteria.

Toast Image Requirements

Toast has multiple storefront products and its documentation can show different specifications for legacy Online Ordering and Online Ordering Pro.

Standard Toast Online Ordering menu items

  • 750 × 450 px rectangle
  • PNG is the safest current recommendation
  • Some Toast pages also state JPG or PNG and a 5 MB maximum

Toast Online Ordering Pro menu items

  • Supports 1:1, 4:3, and 16:9 layouts
  • Minimum width or height of 1000 px recommended
  • The selected layout fills its container, so mismatched photos will crop

Logo, banner, favicon, and eGift

  • Online Ordering Pro supports JPG, JPEG, PNG, WebP, or SVG for logo, favicon, and banner; max 5 MB
  • Legacy guidance may show a square logo around 180–230 px minimum and a banner ranging from 1400 × 788 to 1920 × 1080 depending on product/version
  • eGift card image guidance: minimum 640 × 400 px; max 5 MB

Toast operational warnings

  • Publishing is not instant: Toast says menu images may take at least two hours to appear after saving and publishing.
  • Know which storefront you use: legacy Online Ordering and Online Ordering Pro do not use identical image rules.
  • Menu Builder retirement: Toast states Menu Builder will be retired September 1, 2026; use Menu Manager for ongoing maintenance.
  • Third-party sync: if Toast is your menu source of truth, an image change can flow into connected delivery channels. Check the integration mapping before replacing images in bulk.

Sources: Toast standard Online Ordering, Online Ordering Pro image requirements, and Online Ordering Pro branding.

5

Square Image Requirements

Standard Square item library

  • JPG, JPEG, PNG, or GIF
  • Maximum 15 MB
  • Square 1:1 recommended
  • At least 2000 × 2000 px recommended

Square for Franchises

  • Main and secondary square images: 500 × 500 px
  • Main and secondary rectangular images: 955 × 500 px
  • Cart and modifier images: 500 × 500 px
  • Category icon: 200 × 200 px
  • JPG/JPEG/PNG; up to 2 MB

Square sync and visibility warnings

  • One image follows the item: Square states that an item-catalog image syncs across POS devices and Square Online; you cannot use a different image for the same item on each.
  • Item changes can be global: updates to an item apply wherever that item appears, so confirm every sales channel before editing.
  • Menus and items have separate visibility: assigning a menu to an online or delivery channel does not automatically guarantee every item is enabled for the same location and channel.
  • Bulk image matching can be wrong: Square’s automated matching only attaches images when confidence is high; review matches before saving.
  • Category images may not migrate: Square documents cases where old category images do not sync into the newer Menus dashboard and must be re-added to menu groups.

Sources: Square item images, Square for Franchises, and Square menu channels.

Clover Image Guidance

Clover’s public U.S. pages explain how menus sync across Clover Online Ordering, the Clover app, POS devices, and certain delivery integrations, but they do not currently publish one dependable U.S. image-dimension table for every restaurant setup.

Safest working approach

  • Keep a full-resolution master photo with extra space around the dish
  • Prepare 1:1, 4:3, and 16:9 exports so you can respond to the crop shown in your dashboard
  • Use JPG or PNG unless the upload screen says otherwise
  • Confirm the size and crop in your own Clover dashboard, kiosk, mobile app, and ordering webpage

Clover integration warning

Clover markets a single-menu model in which online-ordering changes sync automatically, and its Grubhub integration can sync the Grubhub menu with Clover inventory. That convenience also means a photo or menu change made at the source can propagate farther than expected. Before replacing images, identify whether Clover, Grubhub, or another middleware provider is the actual source of truth.

Sources: Clover Online Ordering and Clover delivery integrations.

7

ezCater Image Requirements

Banner image

  • Minimum 1200 × 800 px
  • Submit the full-size, uncropped photo
  • Leave generous space around the food because ezCater automatically produces different desktop and mobile crops
  • Food only—avoid text, logos, prep areas, and distracting elements

Logo

  • A horizontal logo uses the available space best
  • Approximately 3:1 is recommended
  • Square or round logos can be used but may appear smaller

Sources: ezCater banner cropping and ezCater logo guidance.

POS and Integration Risks to Check Before Uploading

Run this checklist before changing any restaurant image

  • Find the source of truth: Is the photo controlled in DoorDash/Uber/Grubhub directly, or inherited from Toast, Square, Clover, Olo, Chowly, Deliverect, ItsaCheckmate, or another integrator?
  • Check overwrite direction: Some integrations push POS data outward; others periodically pull marketplace changes back in.
  • Avoid simultaneous edits: Editing the same item in the POS and marketplace during a sync window can cause reversions or duplicates.
  • Test one item first: Replace one low-risk image, publish, and inspect every connected storefront before completing a bulk update.
  • Allow for review and propagation: approval queues, cache delays, and menu publishing can take hours or longer.
  • Do not delete originals: preserve the master and every platform export so you can roll back quickly.
  • Audit every device: desktop, mobile app, mobile web, kiosk, and POS tiles can all use different crops.

Best Cross-Platform Photo Workflow

  1. Shoot or save a high-resolution master with the dish centered and at least 15–20% breathing room.
  2. Create four exports: 1:1, 5:4, 4:3, and 16:9.
  3. Create a separate wide environmental/banner photo rather than stretching a menu-item close-up.
  4. Keep text, logos, utensils, and garnishes out of the crop-risk edges.
  5. Name files consistently: platform-item-placement-dimensions-version.jpg.
  6. Track the upload date, source system, approval status, and every channel where the image appears.

Recommended master-file target

Start with at least 3000 px on the longest side. This is not a universal platform requirement; it is a practical production standard that gives you enough resolution to create the common crops without enlarging a small file.

FAQ

Can one photo work everywhere?

One high-resolution master can be the source, but do not upload the identical crop everywhere. Export versions for 1:1, 5:4, 4:3, and 16:9.

Should menu images include text or prices?

Usually no. Text, promotional claims, prices, watermarks, borders, and coupons increase rejection risk and become outdated quickly.

Why did my photo change after I replaced it?

The image may be controlled by a connected POS or integration, cached by the marketplace, awaiting approval, or automatically cropped for the placement. Check the source-of-truth system first.
